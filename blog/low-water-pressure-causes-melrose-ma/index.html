Low water pressure causes in Melrose, MA homes

Low water pressure in a Melrose home usually comes from one of five places. Think of a clogged aerator or fixture, a partly closed valve, a failing pressure reducing valve, corroded galvanized pipe inside the walls, or a restriction in the service line from the street. Sediment stirred up when the city flushes its water mains can also cut flow until the lines clear.

The fastest way to find the cause is to check how widespread the problem is. One tap points to that fixture. Every tap points to the main valve, the regulator, the service line or the street. The sections below walk through that test in order.

Find the cause in four quick checks

Do these in order. Each takes a few minutes and needs no tools beyond a wrench or pliers.

  1. 1

    Compare hot and cold

    If only hot water is weak, the problem is the water heater, its valves, or hot-side pipes. If both are weak, keep going.

  2. 2

    Check one tap at a time

    Unscrew the aerator from the weak faucet. If flow jumps, the aerator was full of grit or scale.

  3. 3

    Check the main valve

    Find where water enters the house and confirm the valve is fully open. A gate valve stuck half open is common.

  4. 4

    Ask the neighbors

    If the street is affected, the issue is outside your walls. If only you are affected, it is inside the house or in your service line.

Write down what you find. A plumber can diagnose far faster with a clear picture of where flow drops.

Inside-the-house causes in more detail

Most low pressure complaints end up in one of these.

Aerators and fixtures

Mineral grit, scale from a water heater, and flecks from old pipe collect in small screens. Remove, soak in vinegar, rinse, and refit.

Pressure reducing valve

Many homes have a bell-shaped PRV near the main shutoff. When it wears out, pressure drops or swings. Replacement is a standard plumber job.

Galvanized steel pipe

Older homes may still have galvanized supply lines. Rust builds up on the inside wall and narrows the pipe, so flow shrinks even when pressure at the street is fine.

Hidden leak

A leak between the meter and the house reduces pressure at the taps. If the meter spins with everything off, call for leak detection.

If you suspect galvanized pipe, a plumber can often confirm it at the meter or main with a visual check and a magnet test.

What a plumber checks that you cannot easily check

A plumber starts with a gauge on the house side of the main valve and reads static pressure with nothing running. Then the gauge stays on while a few fixtures open, to see how far the number falls. A small drop means the supply is healthy. A big drop means a restriction between the gauge and the street, or inside the house.

Next comes the pressure reducing valve if the house has one. These valves are adjustable and they wear. Sometimes a simple adjustment brings pressure back, and sometimes the valve needs replacing. The plumber also checks the meter side shutoff, any filter or softener on the line, and the water heater inlet valve.

Only after those steps does it make sense to talk about pipe replacement. Opening walls is the expensive part of a plumbing job, so a pressure test that rules out the cheap causes first saves real money.

Melrose water supply and main flushing

Melrose buys its water from the MWRA. The city's Water and Sewer Division operates two water pump stations and reads about 8,500 water meters. The city also flushes its water mains to clear sediment.

The most recent round, in fall 2025, ran from Monday, September 8, to late October, generally 8 am to 4 pm. The city warned that water may look rusty or cloudy, to run cold water until it clears, and to hold off on hot water, laundry and the dishwasher until then. It also said sediment can clog home pipes, reduce pressure or discolor water. If your pressure dipped during flushing, clean your aerators and check the city's current notices for this year's schedule.

When the problem is between the street and your meter

If every fixture is weak and the interior valves are open, the line that brings water into your home is the next suspect. Corrosion, partial blockage, a crushed section or a leak can all choke flow without any sign above ground. Our water service line repair team can test flow at the entry point and tell you whether the line, the valve or the meter connection is the weak link.

If the cause is old galvanized supply pipe inside the walls, spot repairs only buy time. A full or partial repiping project replaces the narrowed runs. Older homes often show the same pattern, so a thorough pressure check can save repeated visits. We also serve Wakefield and Malden.

Do not ignore a sudden drop

A sudden pressure loss with a hissing sound, a wet spot, or a spinning water meter with everything off can mean a leak. Shut off the main valve and call right away. Water in a basement, near a panel or in a ceiling is an emergency until proven otherwise.
