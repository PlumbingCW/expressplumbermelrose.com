Stoneham water and sewer facts that affect your plumbing

MWRA water comes from the Quabbin and Wachusett reservoirs and is treated at the Carroll plant in Marlborough. The treatment includes a monochloramine residual and added corrosion control. If you buy a filter, a fish tank product or rubber replacement parts, check that they are rated for chloramine. Ask us, and we will point you to the right part.

The town runs about 75 miles of water main and replaces roughly a mile a year. In the 2024 report year it replaced 21,275 feet of main, mostly pipe from the 1930s and 1940s, and it flushes the system every year. After street work or flushing, brown or cloudy water at the tap is common. Run a cold tap for a few minutes. If it does not clear, or only one fixture is affected, the cause may be inside your house.

On lead, the 2024 report says Stoneham found 15 lead service lines, removed them, and shows none remaining in town records. Samples from 20 homes came in well under the federal action level. As general advice, lead can still come from old solder and fixtures inside older homes, so ask a plumber about a fixture check if yours is older.

Source type: Stoneham Public Works Water/Sewer Department, 2024 water quality report, and MWRA published information

Sump pumps and the town sewer

Stoneham gets sewer service wholesale from the MWRA, and the town's own department maintains the local sewer lines. The 2024 water report also says the town began Phase 9 of sewer rehabilitation to cut stormwater getting into the sewer. That matters to you because of where your sump pump discharges.

Do this

  • Send sump discharge outside, away from the foundation
  • Test the pump and float each spring
  • Add a battery backup, since Eversource handles outages in town
  • Ask the Building Department if you are unsure of local rules

Avoid this

  • Tying a sump pump or downspout into the sanitary drain
  • Running the pump dry or on a stuck float
  • Ignoring a pump that cycles all day in dry weather
  • Assuming a street backup is your pipe

MWRA says sump pumps and downspouts piped to the sewer are a major source of stormwater inflow, and that such connections are not allowed where separate sanitary sewers exist. We have not found a Stoneham-specific sump rule, so confirm yours with the town. For a new or failing unit, see our sump pump service, and for a recurring backup, a sewer inspection shows what the pipe really looks like.

Permits and the Stoneham Building Department

The Building Department at 35 Central Street enforces the Massachusetts Plumbing Code, 248 CMR, and its page says to call 781-279-2670 for all inspections. Applications are taken through the town's online permit portal.

  1. 1

    Ask before you start

    The town page does not state a rule for water heater swaps, so we confirm the requirement with the office for your job instead of guessing.

  2. 2

    Gas work needs a permit

    Town code says no gas piping or gas system work may begin without a permit from the gas inspector. A gas water heater carries a $45 permit fee under the code, which may be amended, so confirm the current fee.

  3. 3

    Schedule the inspection

    Office hours are Monday, Wednesday and Thursday 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., Tuesday 8 a.m. to 7 p.m., and Friday 8 a.m. to noon.

  4. 4

    Keep the paperwork

    Starting work without a permit can lead to a doubled fee on first notice and a tripled fee after completion. Fees are not refundable. Keep the permit record for a future sale.
