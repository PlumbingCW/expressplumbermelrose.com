Emergency Drain Cleaning in Melrose, MA

A backed-up drain does not wait for business hours, and our 24/7 line does not either. Call for overflowing sinks, tubs that will not empty and floor drains pushing water into the basement. While you wait, stop running water in the house and move anything valuable away from the drain.

Drain cleaning is the removal of a blockage from a household drain line with a cable machine, a drum auger or a water jet. The right tool depends on where the clog sits and what it is made of. A plumber works that out before anything comes off the truck.

Clogged or backed-up drain: what to do first

Stop all water use. Every flush, shower and washer cycle sends more water toward a line that is already full. If only one fixture is slow, a gentle plunger try is fine. If water shows up in a second fixture, the blockage is past the point where the lines join, and plunging will not reach it.

Keep chemical drain openers out of it. They sit in standing water, they burn skin, and they make the job harsher for whoever opens the trap next. If you already poured some in, tell the plumber what it was and when.

Find your cleanout if you can. It is a capped pipe, often in the basement near the main stack or outside by the foundation. Open it only with gloves and a bucket ready, because backed-up water can pour out fast. If you are unsure, leave it for the plumber.

Where the clog sits changes the fix

Kitchen sink

Grease cools and coats the pipe wall, then food catches on it. A sink that fills and drains into the laundry sink usually means the blockage is downstream of where the two lines meet.

Tub or shower

Hair and soap scum build up at the strainer and just below the trap. Water pooling around your feet is the early sign, well before the drain stops.

Basement floor drain

This drain often connects to the main line, so water coming up here can mean a bigger blockage than one fixture. Clear the floor around it and stop water use upstairs.

Washer standpipe

Lint and soap residue narrow the standpipe and the line behind it. An overflowing standpipe mid-cycle means stop the machine, not just mop.

Snake, cable or jet: which method fits which clog

MethodBest forLimit
PlungerOne slow sink or tubDoes not reach past the trap
Drum auger (small snake)Sink and tub clogs in branch linesCan punch a hole through grease and leave the walls coated
Cable machine with cutter headRoots, hair and debris in larger linesNeeds a clear path to a cleanout or fixture
High-pressure water jetGrease and scale on the full pipe wallNeeds sound pipe, see hydro jetting
Camera inspectionClogs that keep coming backShows the cause but does not clear it

If a clog returns within weeks, the line has a cause that clearing alone will not remove. A sewer camera inspection shows a sag, a root intrusion or a cracked joint.

How a drain cleaning call goes

  1. 1

    Scope the problem

    We ask which fixtures are affected and what changed. That tells us whether this is a single branch or the main line.

  2. 2

    Clear the blockage

    The plumber feeds a cable or auger to the clog, breaks it up or retrieves it, then runs the line until it is open.

  3. 3

    Prove the flow

    Water runs at full volume in every fixture on the line. A drain that clears and then slows again is not finished.

  4. 4

    Recommend only what is needed

    If the cause is a damaged or sagging pipe, we say so and show what a camera finds before suggesting repair.

Storm drains, yard drains and area drains

Outdoor drains fill with leaves, silt and roots. A clogged yard drain next to the foundation sends rain toward the wall instead of away from it, so clear it before a forecast storm, not after.

A cable or water jet can clear the pipe running away from a yard or driveway drain. If the line is crushed or has dropped, a camera shows it. For a catch basin in the street, ask the city whether it is theirs before you hire anyone.

If the water from a floor drain or yard line heads for a sewer cleanout, a larger blockage may be involved. Our sewer line cleaning service covers the main line past the house.

Check the faucet aerator before blaming the drain

The City of Melrose's 2023 water quality letter advises removing faucet aerators every few months and running the water for 3 to 5 minutes to reduce lead exposure. The same habit can also clear grit that slows a faucet, because debris caught in the aerator can feel like a drain problem.

Unscrew the aerator, rinse it and see whether the flow returns. If the faucet runs strong but the sink still fills, the clog is in the drain, and that is the call to make. For homes near Malden, the same check applies.
