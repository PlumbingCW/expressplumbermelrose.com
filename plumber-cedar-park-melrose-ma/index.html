Inside your unit or building-wide?

The fastest way to save a bad evening is to decide which column your problem belongs in. These lists are general rules for any multi-unit building.

Usually inside your unit

  • A dripping faucet, running toilet or slow sink in one room
  • A supply line under your sink or behind your washer
  • Your own water heater, if the unit has one
  • A clog that only affects one fixture

Usually building-wide

  • Low pressure or no water in several units at once
  • Gurgling or backups in more than one unit
  • A leak that shows up on a ceiling below you
  • A shared water heater or boiler

A backup in your tub plus a neighbor's slow drain points to the shared line. Our drain cleaning page covers that first step.

Condo association or owner: who handles what

Responsibility for pipes in a condo depends on your governing documents, so we cannot say for you. We can tell you what the questions usually are.

Read the master deed

Many condo documents divide pipes by where they sit, such as inside your walls versus in common walls.

Call management first

For anything shared, tell the association or property manager before work begins.

Renters and landlords

If you rent, report the problem to your landlord first. In a two-family, the owner often arranges repairs for shared lines. Check your lease for who hires the plumber.

For work that is yours, such as a fixture, see faucet repair.

Downtown Main Street and nearby businesses

Downtown Melrose is primarily a commercial district, and the 1981 state survey describes Victorian brick commercial blocks there from the 1880s and 1890s. Where downtown ends and Cedar Park begins is not documented, and we make no claim about which businesses are where. What holds anywhere is that shops, offices and mixed-use buildings have plumbing needs that differ from a house.

If you manage a small commercial space nearby, see our commercial plumbing page and call us to talk through your situation.

Gas smell or sewage backing up

If you smell gas, leave the building right away and call 911 or the gas utility before you call a plumber. Do not use switches, lighters or phones inside.

If sewage is rising in a tub or floor drain, stop using water in the building. Warn neighbors and tell your manager.
