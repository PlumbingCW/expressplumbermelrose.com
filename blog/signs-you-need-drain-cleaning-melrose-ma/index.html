Signs you need your drains cleaned in Melrose, MA

You likely need drain cleaning when a drain is slow in more than one fixture, gurgles after water goes down, smells like sewage, or clogs again within weeks of clearing. One slow sink is usually a local clog. Several slow fixtures at once usually point to the main line.

This guide sorts the warning signs, shows what you can try first and explains when to stop and call a plumber.

Seven warning signs

Each sign tells you something different about where the blockage sits.

Slow drain in one fixture

A single sink or tub that drains slowly usually has hair, soap or grease in its own trap or branch line.

Slow drains in several fixtures

When the shower, sink and toilet all slow down together, suspect the main line.

Gurgling sounds

Air pushed past a blockage makes gurgles, often in a nearby fixture after a flush.

Sewage smell

A persistent odor can mean a dry trap, a vent problem or a partial sewer blockage.

Water backing up elsewhere

Flush the toilet and see water in the tub or floor drain? The main line is blocked.

Repeat clogs

A drain that clogs again within weeks has buildup the first cleaning did not remove.

Wet spots in the yard

Damp ground over the sewer line can mean a break, a root intrusion or a failed joint.

What to try before you call

  1. 1

    Use a plunger

    Seal the overflow with a wet rag, then plunge firmly for 20 to 30 seconds.

  2. 2

    Clear the stopper and trap

    Pull hair from the stopper, then clean the P-trap under the sink with a bucket below.

  3. 3

    Flush with hot tap water

    For soap and grease buildup, run hot water for a few minutes. Do not use boiling water on PVC.

  4. 4

    Try a hand auger

    A small drain snake reaches a few feet into a branch line.

  5. 5

    Stop there

    If two or more fixtures are affected, or water backs up, stop and call.

Skip chemical drain openers. They can damage pipes, they do not cut through tree roots, and they are hazardous to the plumber who works on the line afterward.

Melrose note: city mains and your own pipe

The city maintains its sewer system, and Melrose says it has 74 miles of sewer mains. About 57% of them have been camera inspected, and the city has also lined more than 50,000 linear feet of pipe. Those mains sit in the street.

Your own pipe, from the house to the connection, is a separate question. Who is responsible for what depends on where the problem sits and on your address, so confirm the split with the city before you pay anyone to dig, and tell them what you have already tried. A clog in the street main, if confirmed, is a city matter.

Snaking or hydro-jetting

Cable or snake

  • Punches a hole through the clog
  • Good for hair, paper and simple blockages
  • Costs less and is faster
  • May leave grease on the pipe walls

Hydro-jetting

  • Scours the pipe with high-pressure water
  • Removes grease, scale and light root growth
  • Costs more
  • Suited to repeat clogs and main lines

If a drain clogs twice in a season, jetting is usually the better value. Read about hydro-jetting and how a camera inspection fits in.

When it is the main line

The main line carries everything from the house to the street. When it blocks, the lowest drain in the house gives first: a basement floor drain, a shower or a laundry standpipe. Water backing out of those spots means stop using water at once.

Roots are a common cause in older sewer pipes. Fine roots enter through joints, catch paper and grease, and build a mat that slows flow. Clearing the roots helps for a time, but a camera shows whether the pipe is cracked or offset.

For routine clearing, see our drain cleaning service. Aging cast iron and clay lines can clog in older homes anywhere, and we also serve Malden and Saugus.

Habits that keep drains open

  • ✓
    Use a hair catcher. Hair is the leading cause of shower clogs.
  • ✓
    Keep grease out of the kitchen sink. Wipe pans before washing, and bin the grease.
  • ✓
    Flush only toilet paper. Wipes labeled flushable still clog lines.
  • ✓
    Run hot water after dishes. It moves loose fat along before it sets.
  • ✓
    Schedule a camera check. Older homes benefit from a look before a backup.

What a plumber does when you call

A plumber starts by asking which fixtures are affected, how long it has been going on and whether anything else changed. Those answers narrow the search before any tool comes out.

For a branch clog, a cable machine usually clears it in one visit. For a main line, the plumber may run a camera to see the pipe condition, find the blockage and decide between cleaning and jetting. The camera also shows whether the pipe is cracked, sagging or full of roots.

Ask to see the video. It turns a vague diagnosis into something you can judge for yourself, and it helps if you later need a repair estimate.

Why drains clog in older homes

Older houses often have cast iron or clay pipe with rough inner walls. Grease and scale catch on those walls and narrow the pipe a little more every year. Add a few roots through joints and the line slows long before it stops.

Fixtures change too. A remodeled kitchen with a new disposal sends more food waste down the same old line. The pipe did not change, but the load did, and the first sign is usually a slow drain that keeps coming back.

If you have lived with a slow drain for months, treat it as a sign, not a quirk. Early cleaning is cheaper and easier than clearing a full blockage at night.

What to do while you wait for a plumber

If water is backing up, stop using the fixtures that feed the line. Do not flush, run the washer or take a shower, because every gallon you add has nowhere to go. Move rugs and boxes off the floor near the lowest drain.

Note which fixtures are affected and what you already tried, and tell the plumber when you call. That detail saves time and often lets them bring the right tool on the first trip.
