Sewer camera inspection cost for Melrose, MA homeowners

The cost of a sewer camera inspection varies with access, line length and whether the line needs cleaning first, and it usually rises when the line is clogged or you want the pipe located and a written report. Ask for a written scope and price before work starts. The camera is a small waterproof lens on a flexible rod that records the inside of your sewer line.

The real value is what the footage prevents. A sewer inspection can find a belly, a root intrusion or a cracked section before it becomes a backup, and it gives you something concrete to compare against repair bids. Below are the cost factors, what the video can and cannot show, and the Melrose angles.

What the camera actually shows you

The technician feeds the camera from a cleanout, a pulled toilet or a basement drain and pushes it toward the street. The monitor shows the pipe wall in real time, and a recorder saves the run. Most crews can tell cast iron from clay, plastic or Orangeburg by sight, and they can read the joints.

Common findings include roots entering at joints, offset or separated pipe sections, cracks, scale buildup in old cast iron, low spots where water pools (a belly), and grease or debris buildup. The camera cannot see through a full blockage, so heavy clogs often need cleaning first. That step can add to the bill but gives you a usable picture.

When you get the video, watch the first few feet yourself. Ask the technician to pause on each defect and say how serious it is, and whether it is something to monitor or something to fix this year. A good report separates the two so you do not overspend on a cosmetic crack.

What drives the price

No two lines are alike. These are the factors that move a quote up or down.

Access pointA clean accessible cleanout is quick. Pulling a toilet or working through a hard-to-reach basement drain takes more time.
Line length and depthA longer run to the street means more cable and more footage to review and record.
Pre-cleaningIf the pipe is blocked or coated with grease or roots, the line may need drain cleaning or jetting before the camera can pass.
Locating the problemMarking a defect's depth and surface position with a locator is often priced separately from the basic run.
Report and videoA written summary with stills and a copy of the video adds time, and it matters if you will show it to a seller, buyer or lender.

Melrose note: the city's own sewer camera work and the sale process

Melrose has 74 miles of sewer mains, and the city reports about 57 percent of them, plus 500 manholes, have been inspected with cameras as part of its infiltration and inflow work. That program covers the public main in the street. Your private lateral, the pipe from your house to the main, is a separate question, so ask the city who is responsible for which segment on your lot before you plan a repair.

If you are selling, the city asks the owner to schedule a final water reading, ideally at least 7 days before closing. Staff need access to the property to read and inspect the meter, and a $25 meter read fee is added to the final bill. A camera run and the meter visit can be planned in the same week so nothing delays the closing.

Before the technician arrives

1

Find the cleanout

Look near the main stack in the basement or outside along the foundation. Clear the area so the crew can set up.

2

Skip the heavy flushing

Do not run large loads of water right before the visit. Let the line settle.

3

Note symptoms and dates

Which drains gurgle, when backups happened, and any earlier repairs help the tech read the footage.

4

Have the house plans or age

Pipe material often follows the year built. It helps to know what to expect.

Ask for a copy of the video and a written note on defect locations. You will want both for repair bids.

Do not skip the next step

A camera finds the problem. It does not fix it. Get at least one repair plan in writing, with the scope, whether it is a spot repair or a full replacement, and who pulls any needed permit. Licensed plumbers apply for plumbing permits in Melrose, so that part of the work should not fall on you.

If the camera run finds a collapse or a major belly, sewage backup can follow. Keep sink and tub use light until the repair is done, and call the 24/7 line if water starts backing up.
