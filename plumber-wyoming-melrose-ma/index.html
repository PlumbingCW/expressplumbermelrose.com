Where Wyoming sits and why the housing is mixed

The Massachusetts Historical Commission's 1981 survey of Melrose says the area developed as a suburban railroad depot area after the Boston and Maine line arrived in 1845. It mentions suburban depots on the west side at Wyoming and Melrose Highlands. The MBTA's Wyoming Hill station carries the name today.

Residents define the edges differently, and no official boundary document is known. A public comment on the city's housing plan points to West Wyoming Avenue and to Main Street from Wyoming to Emerson, and Wyoming Cemetery is another reference point. One unofficial data site, using census-tract figures, estimates that 54.1 percent of homes here were built by 1939, with a mix of small to medium units, apartment buildings and single-family houses. If your building holds more than one household, sort out who is responsible before you call.

How to describe the unit and the fixture

In a building with several units, a vague message wastes everyone's time. A short, specific description gets you to the right fix.

    Give the exact unitSay the street number, the unit or floor, and which household called. Mention if the problem seems to come from the unit above or below.
  • ✓
    Name the fixtureKitchen sink, tub, toilet, washing machine hookup or water heater. Say which bathroom or which kitchen.
  • ✓
    Say what you see and hearSlow drain, gurgling, a drip under the sink, no hot water or a wet ceiling below. Note when it started.

Planned work versus a repair

These two kinds of jobs run differently.

Repair now

  • A leak, burst pipe or backed-up drain that is causing damage
  • Close the fixture shutoff or the main, then call
  • Stopping the problem usually comes before planning the full fix

Planned work

  • A water heater swap, new fixtures or a repipe
  • Can be scheduled with the owner, manager and tenants
  • Ask about water heater replacement ahead of time

A repair that keeps recurring is a sign to plan bigger work. A camera look through sewer inspection can show whether a shared line is the cause.

Shared drains, risers and shutoffs

In a building with several units, one drain line often serves more than one household. If a kitchen sink backs up in one apartment and a neighbor's tub is slow too, the clog may sit in a line both use. Drain cleaning or hydro jetting may fix it, though the work can need access to more than one unit.

Shutoffs matter just as much. Each fixture usually has its own valve, and many buildings also have a shutoff for each unit and a main for the whole building.

If water is coming through a ceiling or a sewer smell fills the unit

Stay clear of sagging ceilings and keep out of water near outlets or the panel. Close the shutoff if you can reach it safely, then call your landlord or manager and us.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before anyone calls a plumber.
