Wakefield's water is mostly MWRA water

The town's 2025 water quality report says Wakefield's supply is 94.3 percent MWRA water and 5.7 percent from its own sources, including Crystal Lake. MWRA draws on the Quabbin and Wachusett Reservoirs. For you, that means the water at your tap is largely the same treated water that serves other MWRA towns, and a taste or color change is worth checking against the town report before you blame your pipes.

The report also shows the Broadway Water Treatment Plant was offline in the last two quarters of 2025, so all water came from MWRA in that stretch. The town adds fluoride, and the disinfectant is chloramine or chlorine depending on the source. Disinfectants in tap water can age rubber parts over time, so a toilet that keeps running or a flapper that goes soft is often a routine parts repair.

Lead, service lines and what the town report shows

Wakefield's 2024 lead sampling put the 90th percentile at 2.5 ppb against a 15 ppb action level. One of 30 sampled sites went over, and readings ran from 0.13 to 77.4 ppb. That spread is the point: lead comes from household plumbing and service lines, not from the source water, so one house can differ sharply from its neighbor.

If you got the town's letter

Under the federal Lead and Copper Rule, the town inventoried every service line and mailed letters in November 2024. Lines were sorted into Lead, Galvanized Requiring Replacement or Unknown. If yours says Unknown, a plumber can look at the pipe where it enters the house and tell you what it is.

If the pipe is lead or galvanized

Ask about water service line repair before you buy a filter and stop there. A filter helps at one tap, but the old pipe stays in the ground. We can price the private-side work, and the Water Division can tell you what its part is.

While you decide

Run the cold tap for a minute or two after water has sat for hours. Do not drink or cook with hot tap water. Clean faucet aerators every few months, because they trap debris that loosens when work happens on the line.

When sewage backs up into the house

Wakefield's own guidance is to call the Water and Sewer Division at 781-246-6318 if sewage backs up. The division can determine whether the blockage is in the town system, and that answer tells you whether you are paying for a plumber at all. The town also says to hire a professional cleaning company for the cleanup inside the home.

The town does not state who pays for the private side of a service pipe, so ask the division before you assume. Once the main is cleared, we run a camera and sewer line repair or cleaning on the pipe from your foundation to the street connection.

Heavy rain, sump pumps and the town's sewer

The town's overflow inventory records extremely heavy rain in December 2014 that flooded streets and surcharged sewers, and a rain-related backup into a basement in April 2017. Wakefield's sewer system carries wastewater to the MWRA Deer Island plant, and stormwater that leaks into it raises what the town pays. The town says not to send sump pump or basement flood water to the sanitary sewer and to disconnect roof and outside drains.

So a sump pump in Wakefield should discharge outdoors, not into a floor drain or sewer cleanout. If yours drains to the sewer, that is the first thing to fix. The town also says cracked service pipes let groundwater in, which is why it asks residents to have their sewer service inspected and defects repaired right away.

What the housing mix means for your pipes

The town's May 2025 housing plan says 33 percent of Wakefield's housing units were built in 1939 or earlier and another 26 percent between 1940 and 1959. About 18 percent date from the 1950s alone, well above county and state shares. Pipes from those decades are often galvanized steel, early copper or cast iron, and the original service line may still be in the ground.

The same plan counts 55 percent of units as single-family detached homes and says 71 percent of households own their home. That is why we lead with repair of the whole path from meter to fixture. If a house shows rust-colored water, repeated pinhole leaks or low pressure at several taps, ask us about repiping rather than patching one leak at a time.

Gas smell or hissing: leave first

Wakefield Municipal Gas and Light says to leave the home immediately if you think you smell gas, avoid anything that could spark, such as light switches and phones, and call the utility from a neighbor's home. Do not call us from inside the house and do not wait for a response time. After the utility clears the home, we handle repairs on gas lines and appliances.

Before we arrive in Wakefield

A few minutes of prep saves time at the door. These steps fit the older owner-occupied homes common here.

1

Find and close the main valve

In many older houses it sits near the meter in the basement. Turn it clockwise until it stops, then open one tap low in the house to drain pressure.

2

Stop using fixtures if drains back up

Every flush or load of laundry sends more water toward the blockage. Hold off until the town or we have checked the line.

3

Photograph the problem

Take pictures of the water, the pipe and the water heater label. Your insurer may ask for them, and we use them to bring the right parts.

4

Clear a path

Move boxes from the basement stairs and around the heater, panel and floor drain. Older cellars are tight, and clear space speeds the work.

If you have a letter from the town about your service line, have it handy.
