Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Melrose, MA

No water at every tap, or a swampy patch between the street and your foundation, usually means the service line has failed. Our 24/7 line takes emergency water service line repair calls in Melrose. While you wait, close the main valve inside the house and keep kids and pets off soft ground.

The water service line is the buried pipe that carries water from the main in the street, through the meter, into your home. When it cracks, corrodes or separates at a joint, you lose pressure, water or both.

No water or a broken line from the street

Before you assume the service line is the problem, check the simple things. Ask a neighbor whether their water is off too, since a main break or city work can shut a whole block. Look at the meter and the main valve to confirm both are open.

If the neighbors are fine and your valves are open, the line is the likely cause. A partial break gives weak pressure and a wet yard. A full break can mean no water at all. Either one is worth a call tonight, because a leaking line also undermines the soil under driveways and walks.

Who is responsible for which part

In general, the city owns the main in the street, and the homeowner is responsible for part of the line that runs onto private property. Where the split falls is local, so confirm it before you approve work.

Lead service lines and the Melrose program

MWRA water leaves the reservoirs lead free, and the city says its own pipes are mostly iron and steel and add no lead. Lead can enter from a home's lead service line, lead solder or some brass fixtures. In the 2023 report, Melrose's 90th percentile for lead was 16.3 ppb against a 15 ppb action level. Check the city's latest water quality letter for current results.

Under a voluntary MassDEP consent order, Melrose must remove at least 25 complete lead service connections each year, and it completed 34 in 2023. The city also offers a $1,000 incentive to owners who replace a private-side lead service. Call the Melrose Engineering Division before you hire any replacement, since the incentive and the city's own schedule can affect how and when you do the work.

Until the line is checked, flush the tap 30 seconds to 2 minutes after water has sat for hours, and at least a minute if it sat more than 6. Take the aerators off every few months and run the water 3 to 5 minutes. The city says never use hot tap water for drinking or cooking, and never boil water to remove lead.

How a service line repair goes

  1. 1

    Confirm and locate

    We verify the loss with a meter and pressure check, then pinpoint the break with listening equipment so we dig in one spot, not along the whole run.

  2. 2

    Plan and notify

    We mark the route, confirm utilities before digging, and coordinate any city sign-off or permit the work needs.

  3. 3

    Repair or replace

    A single break can be cut out and spliced. A corroded or lead line is usually replaced end to end with new pipe.

  4. 4

    Test and restore

    We pressure-test the new pipe, restore water, flush the line and refill the trench. Surface restoration is a separate item.

The city says sub-trade permits are granted only to licensed professionals, and Melrose plumbing permits are applied for online. Ask who is pulling the permit before work starts.

Digging a trench or going trenchless

Open trench

  • Straight, visible access to the whole pipe
  • Easiest for a short run or a single repair
  • More lawn, walk or pavement to restore
  • Shows lead or old steel clearly

Trenchless methods

  • Pulls or pushes new pipe through the old path
  • Fewer and smaller holes
  • Less yard damage, good under drives and walks
  • Not right for every pipe or soil

Which method fits depends on pipe condition, depth, and what sits above the line. We explain the choice and the surface work it leaves before you approve.

Safety near a failing service line

Stay off soft or sunken ground over the pipe route, since saturated soil can give way. Keep children and pets away from open trenches and from water pooling near the foundation. Never dig near the street connection yourself, because gas, electric and communication lines often run close to water lines.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility first, before you call a plumber.
