Sewer backup: what to do first

Stop using water everywhere in the house. That includes flushing, the washer and the dishwasher, because every gallon has nowhere to go but the lowest drain. Keep family and pets away from any water that came up through a drain.

Open windows for ventilation and avoid touching sewage without gloves. If water is near outlets or appliances, do not stand in it. Turn off power to that area only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.

Call us, then take photos of the damage before cleanup for your insurer. A plumber will confirm with a camera whether the backup is a clog or a broken pipe, and that decides whether you need sewer line cleaning or a repair.

What breaks in a sewer line

Most sewer repairs trace back to one of a few problems. The question is which one, and a camera answers it before any digging starts.

  • Roots that enter through joints and thicken until the pipe is blocked or split
  • Cracked or offset clay pipe, where sections have shifted and left a lip that catches debris
  • A bellied section, where the pipe sags and holds water and solids
  • Corroded cast iron, which flakes inside and eventually cracks or collapses
  • Crushed pipe from heavy loads, shifting soil or past excavation

The camera footage, not a guess, picks the method. Many lines need a mix, such as a short dug repair at a collapse and lining for the rest.

Trenchless lining and cast iron pipe repair

Pipe lining

Lining, called cured-in-place pipe or CIPP, pushes a resin-soaked sleeve into the old pipe and cures it into a smooth new pipe inside the old one. It avoids a long trench through your lawn, driveway or finished basement. The cost is a slightly smaller inside diameter and the need for a pipe that is still open enough to take the liner.

The City of Melrose has used CIPP for more than 50,000 linear feet of its public sewer mains, so the method is established locally. Lining your private line is a separate job, and a camera has to show the pipe is a candidate first.

Cast iron

Old cast iron inside a basement or under a slab corrodes from the inside. A plumber can cut out and replace a failed section or line it, depending on how much of the pipe is sound. Cast iron carries a lot of noise, so a replacement section often changes how quiet the drains are as well.

Permits and inspections for a Melrose sewer job

Plumbing permits in Melrose are applied for online through the city's SimpliCity portal. The city says staff review can take 2 to 3 weeks depending on volume, so ask early who is pulling the permit and how timing works for an urgent repair. The printed permit must be posted at the job site.

Plumbing inspections are scheduled by calling Inspectional Services between 12 pm and 1 pm on the day you want, and they run Monday to Thursday only. A reinspection costs $50, and work started without a permit costs triple the fee. The city says sub-trade permits are granted only to licensed professionals. Ask us who is pulling the permit before work starts.
