The plumbing problems older buildings produce

Census Reporter counts 36,792 housing units in Malden (ACS 2024 one-year estimate). Where a building holds more than one unit, one failure can reach several households. A leaking stack or a clogged building drain rarely stays in one apartment.

The city also says its water and sewer systems have served the community since the late 1800s. Old mains and old house laterals are where roots, scale, offsets and grease buildups tend to show up. If a basement drain gurgles or a toilet backs up after heavy use, start with a camera look instead of guessing. Our sewer inspection work shows what the line actually looks like before anyone digs.

Renters also outnumber owners here. Census QuickFacts puts the owner-occupied rate at 41.3 percent. If you manage or rent a unit, document the problem with photos and tell the owner right away, because a slow leak inside a wall gets worse every day it sits.

Problems we see calls for in older Malden buildings

These are the usual suspects in older housing. None of them is unique to one street, and each one has a quick first step.

    Main line backups. Slow drains in several fixtures at once point to the shared line, not a single trap. Stop running water and call.

Lead service lines and what Malden homeowners should check

In the 2024 report, Malden's fall 2024 lead 90th percentile was 19.2 ppb, above the 15 ppb action level, and the city's letter states Malden was the only MWRA community to exceed it that year. The regional supply is treated, so the concern is old service lines and old fixtures inside buildings. Spring 2025 sampling came back at 8.4 ppb.

The city's own guidance gives a simple first check. Lead service lines are generally dull gray and very soft, and a light scratch with a key shows bright silver if it is lead. Do not use a knife or sharp tool. The city also offers a free lead water test, and inspection requests go through its SeeClickFix portal.

If you find lead on the private side, the city's ordinance page says the owner is responsible for removing it and replacing it with copper. If the lead is on the public side, the city adds the property to its replacement list. Low-interest loans exist for eligible owners replacing the private portion, so ask the city before you budget. Our water service line repair work covers the private-side pipe from the shutoff to the house.

Private side or city side: how to read the problem

Sorting out the source early keeps you from paying for work that is not yours, or waiting on a fix that is.

Likely your pipe

  • Only one building backs up while neighbors are fine
  • Water is entering through the basement floor drain
  • The fixture trouble began after a recent plumbing change
  • Water pressure drops only inside the house

Likely the city main

  • Several neighboring homes lose pressure at once
  • Water in the street or a sinkhole forming near the curb
  • A sewer smell comes from the street drain
  • Low water pressure on the whole block

Even when the cause is the city's, a plumber can confirm your side is clear. A quick sewer line cleaning pass often settles the question.

Wet basements, sump pumps and where the water goes

The city's stormwater page says the drainage system carries stormwater to the Malden River or the Pines River, and that storm drains in shallow groundwater areas may help keep groundwater from seeping into basements. That is useful context, but it does not guarantee a dry cellar.

The MWRA notice sent to Malden residents advises connecting sump pumps and roof drains to a storm drain or dry well, not the sanitary sewer, because extra clean water can overload the sewer. If your pump discharges into a basement drain, ask a plumber to look at the routing. Our sump pump service covers testing, replacing and rerouting the discharge line.

Soft water means fewer scale problems

MWRA reports its water hardness at about 16 mg/L, roughly one grain per gallon, which counts as soft. In practice, tanks and fixtures here see less scale than in hard-water towns, so most Malden homes do not need a softener. What you should watch for instead is corrosion in old lead or copper plumbing, which is why the system adjusts pH to reduce it.

Permits, landlords and what to expect from the city

Malden Inspectional Services handles plumbing and gas permits, and the city states that only licensed electricians and plumbing contractors can apply for plumbing and gas permits online. That means a homeowner generally routes a permit through the plumber. The fee schedule lists water heaters at $50 each for electric or gas, and work started without a permit costs triple the original permit fee. Ask your plumber about the permit before work starts.

Landlords have a second reason to keep plumbing in order. The Health Department runs a rental unit inspection under the State Sanitary Code, and plumbing condition can come up in a dwelling inspection, so ask the Health Department what it checks. If a unit's water heater is failing, our water heater replacement service covers the swap, and the permit question goes to Inspectional Services before work starts.

One more rule that matters to sellers and owners of multi-family property. The city's lead pipe ordinance requires owners to certify lead-free service on property sale, on a building permit over $30,000 and on a tenant transfer in residential multi-family property. The certification fee is $25. Plan for it before you list.
