Is it easy to replace a water heater? What Melrose, MA homes need to know

Replacing a water heater is not easy in the way that swapping a faucet is easy. The tank is heavy, the connections involve gas or high-amp electric, the venting must be right to keep carbon monoxide out of the house, and the relief valve must be piped correctly. In Melrose the city publishes a permit fee for water heaters, and it says plumbing and gas permits go only to licensed professionals. Ask the city whether your replacement needs one.

Below is why each part is harder than it looks, what a professional does in order, how long the job generally takes, and the permit fee lines the city has published. Use it to decide whether to hire or to know what to ask when you do.

The short answer for a homeowner

Disconnecting a tank is the easy part. Getting a gas connection leak-free, a vent drafting correctly, a relief valve discharging to a safe place, and the whole job inspected is the hard part. Mistakes there do not show up as a drip. They show up as gas odor, flue gas in the house, scalding water, or a tank that fails under pressure.

What makes each part of the job risky

Here is where DIY water heater swaps go wrong.

Gas connectionLoose fitting or no leak testGas leak and fire risk

Each row is a separate skill. A professional checks all of them on every job.

Before the plumber arrives

A few minutes of prep makes the job go faster.

1

Clear the area

Move boxes and anything stored within a few feet of the heater. The installer needs room to work and to remove the old tank.

2

Find the main shutoffs

Know where the water shutoff, the gas shutoff, and the electrical breaker for the heater are.

3

Plan for no hot water

Fill a pot or two for the morning. Many installs take part of a day, and the tank must be filled and heated.

4

Note your tank details

Photograph the label with size, fuel type and age. It helps the plumber bring the right parts.

If you smell gas, do not touch switches. Leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first.

What a professional does, in order

This is the typical sequence for a tank replacement.

  1. 1

    Shut off and drain

    Fuel and water are shut off, power is isolated, and the tank is drained to a floor drain or outside.

  2. 2

    Remove the old unit

    Flue, gas line, water lines and T&P discharge are disconnected, and the tank is hauled out.

  3. 3

    Set and connect the new one

    Pan, tank, vent, gas and water lines go in. New flex connectors and valves are usually part of the job.

  4. 4

    Pipe the safety devices

    The T&P valve is fitted with a full-size discharge pipe that ends in a safe spot, and an expansion tank is added where needed.

  5. 5

    Test and inspect

    Gas joints are leak tested, the vent is checked for draft, the tank is filled and fired, and any inspection the city requires is booked.

Where space and fuel allow, many homeowners compare a standard tank to a tankless unit during this decision. That changes the scope of the job and the price.

Melrose permits and inspections for water heaters

Melrose Inspectional Services, on the ground floor of City Hall, issues plumbing and gas permits. The city's fee schedule lists a $40 water heater plumbing fee and a $40 residential fee for a gas water heater, plus a $15 administration fee per application. A reinspection costs $50, and work started without a permit is charged triple.

Permits are applied for online through the city's SimpliCity portal. The city says staff review can take 2 to 3 weeks depending on volume, the printed permit must be posted at the job site, and sub-trade permits are issued only to licensed professionals. Plumbing and gas inspections run Monday to Thursday and are scheduled by phone between noon and 1 pm on the day wanted. After-hours inspections carry a separate fee. Ask your plumber how permit timing works for an urgent replacement.

Source type: city website

What affects the price of a replacement

Prices vary by home and by tank, so here are the factors rather than a quote.

Fuel type.Gas, electric and oil units have different parts and venting needs. Gas adds a vent and gas connection that electric does not.
Tank size and efficiency.Larger and higher-efficiency tanks cost more to buy and may need a different vent.
Venting changes.Moving from a chimney vent to a sidewall power vent adds parts and labor.
Code upgrades.Pans, expansion tanks, new valves and a corrected discharge pipe add to the work if the old setup lacks them.
Access and haul-away.Basement stairs, tight closets and a heavy old tank all add time.

How long it takes and what else to consider

A like-for-like tank replacement is commonly done in a few hours once the installer is on site. Venting changes, new gas piping or a tankless conversion take longer. Time to heat a full tank is extra, so plan for hot water to return later the same day rather than immediately.
