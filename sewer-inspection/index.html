Sewer Camera Inspection in Melrose, MA

A sewer camera inspection sends a waterproof camera through your sewer line and records what it sees, so you see the pipe instead of guessing. It shows roots, cracks, sags, offset joints and blockages, and it tells you how far from the house each one sits.

Why look inside the line at all

A sewer line is buried and rarely fails in a way you can see. By the time a drain backs up, the pipe may have been damaged for years. A camera finds the problem before it turns into a flooded basement.

The inspection also shows what you do not need. A line with a light mineral buildup may need cleaning, not repair. A line with one cracked joint may take a spot repair, not full replacement.

When a camera inspection makes sense

Before buying

Older homes, clay or cast iron pipe and large trees nearby are reasons to check the line before closing. The video becomes part of your negotiation.

After repeat clogs

If a line backs up more than once, the cause is in the pipe. A camera shows roots, a sag or a crack so you fix the cause, not the symptom.

Before a project

Digging a patio, a new driveway or an addition over the line? Knowing where it runs and what shape it is in avoids a broken pipe under new concrete.

After cleaning

A pass after a cleaning or jetting shows whether the line is truly clear and what caused the blockage.

Unexplained smells or wet spots

A sewer smell or soft ground over the line can mean a leak. The camera tells you if the pipe is leaking.

How an inspection works

  1. 1

    Find an access point

    We use a cleanout when there is one. If not, the camera goes in through a pulled toilet or a roof vent.

  2. 2

    Run the camera to the street

    A flexible rod pushes the camera through the line while it records. The screen shows the pipe live.

  3. 3

    Mark the problem spots

    A locator at the camera tip lets us find the point on the surface and measure depth and distance.

  4. 4

    Review the video

    You see the footage and hear what is serious, what can wait and what needs nothing at all.

What a camera report cannot tell you

A camera shows the inside of the pipe on the day of the visit. It cannot predict when roots will return or promise a pipe will last. It also cannot see through a blockage, so heavy backups may need cleaning before the camera can run the full length.

Think of the video as a baseline. A second look in a couple of years shows whether buildup or roots are coming back faster than expected.

Buying or selling in Melrose

When a Melrose home is sold, the owner schedules a final water reading, ideally at least 7 days before closing, and a $25 meter read fee is added to the final bill. Buyers can use that same window to book a camera inspection so repair findings do not land on closing day.

The City of Melrose has camera-inspected about 57 percent of its 74 miles of sewer mains as part of its infiltration and inflow work. That work is on the city's mains. It does not show the condition of the pipe serving your house, so ask whether your home inspection includes a sewer camera. Ask for the footage whichever side you are on.
