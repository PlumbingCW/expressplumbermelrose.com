Disposal jammed, leaking or humming: first steps

Cut power at the wall switch and the breaker. A disposal can restart with a jolt when a jam releases, and the blades do not care that you only wanted to check.

Use tongs or pliers to pull out anything visible, never fingers. If the unit hums but does not spin, it is jammed. If water drips from the bottom or the side, the housing or a seal has failed, and a repair rarely beats a replacement at that point.

Close the valves under the sink if it is leaking, and put a bowl under the drip until we arrive.

How we sort out a disposal problem

  1. 1

    Power off, then look

    We confirm the breaker is off and check the unit, the dishwasher hose and the drain connections for the real source of the leak.

  2. 2

    Free the jam

    A hex wrench in the socket at the bottom of the unit turns the plate by hand. Pressing the red reset button resets an overheated motor.

  3. 3

    Test the drain

    If the basin still holds water after the unit spins, the clog is in the pipe, not the grinder. We clear it with a drain cleaning tool.

  4. 4

    Repair or replace

    We tell you which parts are failing and quote both options before any work begins.

What each symptom usually means

Hums but does not spin

A spoon, bone or fruit pit is wedged against the plate, or the motor is seized. Freeing it with a hex wrench usually works. A motor that stays dead after a reset needs replacement.

Leaks from the bottom

Water at the base means a cracked housing or a failed internal seal. This is the most common reason we replace a unit instead of repairing it.

Leaks at the top or sides

The sink flange, drain gasket or discharge connection has loosened. Often a new gasket and tightened fittings stop it.

Sink fills and will not drain

The clog may sit past the unit in the trap or the branch line. If both sides of a double sink fill, the line behind the wall is blocked.

Dishwasher line popped loose

The hose clamp on the disposal inlet can slip. On new units, the dishwasher knockout plug must be punched out or the dishwasher will not drain.

Repair or replace a disposal

Repair when

  • The unit is jammed but the motor spins freely once cleared.
  • The leak is at the sink flange, a gasket or the drain connection.
  • The dishwasher hose simply needs reattaching.
  • The unit is fairly new and sized right for the household.

Replace when

  • Water leaks from the housing or the bottom of the unit.
  • The motor hums and the reset button does nothing.
  • The grinder is rusted, loose or throws out black residue.
  • Your household grinds more than the old unit can handle.

Residential disposals commonly run from 1/3 to 1 horsepower. A larger motor grinds tougher scraps and tends to jam less, but it needs more room under the sink.
