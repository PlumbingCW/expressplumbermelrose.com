Emergency Water Heater Repair in Melrose, MA

Water heater repair means finding why a tank or tankless unit stopped heating, started leaking or began making noise, then fixing that part or telling you straight when the unit is finished. If water is pooling around the heater or the hot tap has gone cold, call our 24/7 line and a dispatcher will take the details and send a technician.

While you wait, shut off the cold water supply to the heater and turn off the gas control or the breaker. Those two steps stop most damage before anyone arrives.

Leaking tank or no hot water: do this while you wait

A leaking water heater can release a lot of water quickly, so the first few minutes matter. Work through these in order and stop if anything feels unsafe.

  1. 1

    Close the cold supply

    Find the shutoff valve on the pipe feeding the top of the heater and turn the handle clockwise until it stops. If the handle is stuck, close the main water valve instead.

  2. 2

    Cut the fuel or power

    On a gas unit, turn the gas control knob to pilot or off. On an electric or hybrid unit, switch off its breaker at the panel. Never leave an electric element powered with an empty tank.

  3. 3

    Protect the floor

    Put towels or a bucket in the path of the water and lift boxes or stored items off the floor. A wet vacuum helps if you own one.

  4. 4

    Smell for gas

    If you smell gas at any point, leave the building, do not touch switches, and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Call us after that.

  5. 5

    Call and describe what you see

    Tell the dispatcher where the water is coming from, the fuel type and roughly how old the heater is. That lets the technician bring the right parts.

If the water is hot, keep children and pets away from the puddle. Do not open the drain valve unless you know where the hose will run.

What the symptom usually means

Most water heater calls fall into a handful of patterns. Here is how we read them at the door.

Water at the base

A drip from a fitting, the relief valve or the drain valve can often be repaired. Water seeping from the tank body itself, or rust streaks on the jacket, points to a failed tank.

No hot water at all

On gas units we check the pilot, thermocouple and gas supply. On electric units we check the breaker, reset button, thermostats and heating elements.

Lukewarm or runs out fast

A broken dip tube, a failed lower element or heavy sediment all shorten the hot supply. Each has a different fix, so the test comes first.

Popping or rumbling

Those sounds are usually minerals baked onto the tank bottom or the element, with water trapped under them. Flushing helps early on. Thick buildup may not come out.

Gas, electric and hybrid water heater repair

The fuel type decides what fails and what a repair visit looks like. A hybrid is an electric tank with a heat pump on top, so it shares electric faults and adds its own.

Electric tankFailed heating element, bad thermostat, tripped high-limit reset, tripped breakerBreaker, reset button, then element and thermostat readings

Fault lists describe common patterns, not a diagnosis. Your unit's model and age change the answer.

Gas appliance work is safety work. A flame that burns yellow, a sooty burner or a draft problem is not a do-it-yourself job. Our tankless water heater service page covers on-demand units in more detail.

Pilot, igniter, sediment and the relief valve

On older gas heaters, a pilot that keeps going out is usually a worn thermocouple or a dirty pilot tube. Newer models use an electronic igniter instead, and a bad igniter or flame sensor shows up as a clicking sound with no flame. Both are small parts and quick to replace.

Sediment is the other steady cause of trouble. Minerals settle at the bottom of any storage tank over time, and the burner or element then works through a layer of crust. The Melrose water report from MWRA gives no hardness figure, so the only way to know how fast your tank collects scale is to look at what comes out of the drain valve during a flush.

The temperature and pressure (T&P) relief valve is a safety part. It opens if the tank gets too hot or the pressure climbs too high, and it should have a discharge pipe running toward the floor. If that pipe is dripping, the valve may be worn or the home may have a pressure problem, such as a closed system without an expansion tank. Never cap the valve or plug the pipe.

Repair or replace: a rough decision table

Tank life varies a lot by brand, water and use. As a general rule of thumb, a storage tank lasts about 8 to 12 years, and this table uses that range only as a starting point.

A leaking tank body cannot be repaired

Once water escapes through the steel shell, the tank lining has failed. Patching it does not work, and running it risks a larger flood. We shut it down, drain it if needed and move to a replacement plan.
