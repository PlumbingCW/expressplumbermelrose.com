Sump pump failed during a storm

Pumps fail when you need them most, because heavy rain is when they run hardest. A stuck float, a burned motor, a clogged intake or a power outage can all stop the pump while water keeps coming in.

Check the simple things first, but only if it is safe. Is the pump plugged in, and is the breaker live? If the pit is full and the pump hums without moving water, call. If water is near electrical equipment, stay out and call.

Water, power and the basement

Never step into standing water that touches outlets, a panel or appliances, even if you think the power is off. If the electrical panel is dry and you can reach it without crossing water, turn off the breakers for that area. If you cannot, leave it for the utility or an electrician.

Sewage ejector pits carry waste, so avoid contact and keep children and pets away. Open a window if there is a smell. Photograph the water level and damage for your insurer.

Sump pump or sewage ejector

Sump pump

  • Removes groundwater and rain seepage from a pit
  • Handles clear water and small silt
  • Discharge goes outside or to an approved drain
  • Uses a float switch to turn on

Sewage ejector

  • Lifts wastewater from a basement bathroom or laundry up to the sewer line
  • Handles solids
  • Sits in a sealed, vented basin
  • Failure can cause a sewage backup

Which one you have changes the repair and the urgency. A failed ejector can mean sewage, so read about sewer line cleaning if waste is coming up.

How a pump repair or replacement goes

  1. 1

    Find the failure

    The plumber checks power, float, switch, check valve and discharge to see whether the pump or something else stopped.

  2. 2

    Clear the pit

    Debris, silt and stones that block the intake or jam the float come out of the basin.

  3. 3

    Repair or replace

    A pump that still has life may need only a new float or valve. An old or burned-out pump gets replaced.

  4. 4

    Test under load

    Water goes into the pit to prove the pump turns on, empties the basin and shuts off cleanly.

Where the pump water goes

The discharge line decides whether your pump keeps working, and where it ends matters. The City of Melrose has spent more than $9 million on infiltration and inflow work which targets clean water getting into sewer pipes. For you, that means do not assume you can connect a sump discharge to the sanitary sewer.

The city's code, Chapter 228 (Water, Sewers, and Drains), has sections titled Separating surface water from wastewater and Discharge of clean water to sanitary sewers. Ask the city what they require before you connect or keep a sump discharge to the sewer.

If the answer is to discharge outside, a plumber can route the line away from the foundation so the water does not circle back to the pit. For help with drains outside the house, see drain cleaning.
