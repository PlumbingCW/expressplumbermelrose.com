Plumber in Melrose Highlands, Melrose, MA

If you need a plumber in Melrose Highlands, the house itself is part of the story. The Massachusetts Historical Commission's 1981 survey of Melrose describes blocks of suburban houses from the 1890s and early 1900s in this part of the city. A house of that age may have been repaired and updated many times, so its plumbing may not all be one age.

This page covers what to know before a plumbing job in an older house, and how to describe a problem when you call. It is general information. We never assume what is inside your walls until someone has looked.

What the Highlands name points to

Residents draw the edges of Melrose Highlands in different ways, and no city document fixes them. What the record does show is a set of anchors. The Melrose Highlands MBTA station carries the name, and the 1981 state survey names Vinton Street and Franklin Street as part of the Highlands. The survey also notes Craftsman houses from around 1910 on Franklin Street.

At an open house for the Melrose Housing Production Plan, 30 respondents, the largest group, listed Melrose Highlands as their neighborhood. The practical point: when you call, give your street and cross street, not just the neighborhood name.

Age tells you where to look, not what you will find

A house built around 1900 can hold pipe from several decades. A bathroom added in the 1950s and a water heater swapped last year can sit in the same building. Nobody can say from the street what is behind a given wall.

A good plumber starts with the exposed pipe, the fixtures and the symptoms, then explains what that shows. If a job needs a camera or a small opening in a wall, ask for the reason first. Age is a reason to inspect, not to assume the worst.

Keeping older fixtures versus replacing them

Older fixtures are not automatically a problem. Some only need a washer or cartridge, while others cost more to keep running than to replace.

Often worth repairing

  • A faucet with a single drip and parts still available
  • A toilet that runs because of a worn flapper or fill valve
  • A shutoff valve that only needs a new packing nut

Often worth replacing

  • A fixture that leaks again after repeated repairs
  • One where parts can no longer be found
  • A valve that will not close fully or crumbles when turned

For one dripping tap, see our faucet repair page. If you are unsure, ask for both options in plain terms.

Planning a water heater replacement

The tank is the appliance most homeowners have to plan around. It is easier to choose well before it fails.

  1. 1

    Read the label

    Note the size, fuel type and the manufacture date if printed. A rusty base or damp floor is a warning sign.

  2. 2

    Check the space

    Measure the area and look at the venting, shutoffs and drain nearby. Tight corners shape which unit fits.

  3. 3

    Compare the options

    A tank and a tankless unit change the piping and venting. Our water heater replacement page covers the choices.

  4. 4

    Ask about permits

    Requirements vary, so ask Melrose Inspectional Services what applies to your project. We will not guess at a rule.

Drain care in an older house

Keep grease, coffee grounds and wipes out of older drains, and use a strainer in the tub and kitchen sink. Harsh chemical cleaners can be hard on pipes and fittings of any age.

If a plunger fails, stop and call. When several fixtures are slow, the cause is often the main line, a different job from one sink. You can see all of our service areas or return to the Melrose home page any time.

When to stop and shut the water off

If water is spraying or pooling, close the main shutoff and open a low faucet to relieve pressure. Keep clear of water near the electrical panel.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility before anyone else. Sewer smell with rising water in a basement drain means stay out of the area and call.
