Garbage disposal installation in Melrose, MA: DIY or plumber?

Replacing an existing garbage disposal with the same style is a reasonable DIY job for a handy homeowner. Installing a first disposal under a sink that never had one is not, because it adds electrical work, a drain change and a dishwasher connection that can fail quietly. The deciding question is what is already under your sink.

This guide gives you a decision table, the order of a safe swap, the dishwasher knockout step that catches people out, and a list of what should never go down a disposal. It also covers what Melrose homeowners should check with the city before bigger plumbing work.

Swap or new install: the first decision

Start by looking under the sink. If a disposal is already there, you are replacing a known setup. If there is only a plain drain, you are building a new one.

Swap an existing disposal

  • Power is already wired or plugged in at the sink cabinet
  • Drain outlet and dishwasher hose are already in place
  • Mounting ring usually matches within the same brand
  • Most work is lifting, twisting and reconnecting

First-time install

  • May need a new outlet or switched circuit
  • Sink flange and trap often need reworking
  • Dishwasher hose needs a high loop or air gap
  • A mistake can mean a leak inside the cabinet for weeks

If your situation reads like the second column, hire the job out. The electrical part is the one most likely to be done wrong by a first-timer.

How a safe disposal swap goes

These are the steps for replacing an existing unit. Skip none of them.

  1. 1

    Cut the power

    Unplug the unit or turn off its breaker. Confirm with a tester or by trying the switch. A disposal that spins during removal causes injuries.

  2. 2

    Disconnect the drain and hose

    Put a bucket under the trap. Loosen the drain outlet and the dishwasher hose clamp. Expect old water.

  3. 3

    Release the mounting ring

    Support the unit with one hand, then twist the ring to unlock it. Disposals are heavy.

  4. 4

    Mount the new unit

    Clean the sink flange, reseal with plumber's putty if needed, and lock the new unit to the ring.

  5. 5

    Reconnect and test

    Reattach the drain and dishwasher hose, restore power, run water, and check every joint with a dry paper towel.

Run the unit with cold water, then fill the sink and let it drain. A slow leak shows up under load, not during a quick test.

The dishwasher knockout: the step people miss

Most disposals have a small plug on the dishwasher inlet. It must be knocked out if a dishwasher drains through the unit. It must stay in if no dishwasher is connected.

Knock it out only if needed

If your dishwasher drains into the disposal, remove the plug with a screwdriver and hammer. Then fish the plug piece out of the chamber, or it will jam the unit or block the dishwasher.

Leave it if there is no dishwasher

With no dishwasher, the plug stays. Removing it creates a hole that leaks every time the sink drains.

Check the hose loop

The dishwasher drain hose should loop high under the counter, or run through an air gap, so dirty water cannot flow back into the dishwasher.

If you discover the plug was left in after the dishwasher stops draining, the fix is small but messy. It is a common reason for a call-back.

What not to put in a disposal

A disposal is a chopper with a small motor. It is not a trash can. These items cause most clogs and failures.

  • ✓
    Grease, oil and fat. They coat the trap and drain pipe, then cool and harden. This is the leading cause of kitchen drain clogs.
  • ✓
    Fibrous food. Celery, corn husks, onion skins and artichoke leaves wrap around the shredder and stall the motor.
  • ✓
    Coffee grounds in bulk. Small amounts pass. Large amounts settle in the trap and become paste.
  • ✓
    Starchy foods. Rice, pasta and potato peels swell with water and turn gluey.
  • ✓
    Bones and pits. Large bones, fruit pits and shells can dull or jam the grinding parts.
  • ✓
    Non-food items. Glass, plastic, cigarette butts and utensils. If a spoon drops in, shut off power before reaching in.

Run cold water before, during and for about 15 seconds after grinding. Cold water keeps any fat firmer so it moves down the pipe instead of smearing on the walls.

Permits and city contacts for kitchen plumbing in Melrose

Plumbing permits in Melrose are issued by Inspectional Services at City Hall and are applied for online through the city's SimpliCity portal. The city says sub-trade permits are granted only to licensed professionals. Its published remodeling fee covers the first four fixtures at $75, with $10 for each additional one.

Ask Inspectional Services whether replacing a disposal with an identical unit needs a permit. If you are moving drains, adding a new sink bowl or doing the work as part of a kitchen remodel, ask Inspectional Services before you cut pipe. Also ask your hired plumber who files the permit.

When a disposal problem is really a drain problem

A slow sink or a gurgle after you run the disposal often points to the trap or the branch line, not the motor. A disposal that hums but will not turn is stuck. A sink full of standing water with a working disposal is a blockage downstream. For that, drain cleaning clears the line, while our garbage disposal service handles replacement and repair of the unit itself.

Kitchen clogs from grease and starches piling up in a trap are common in older kitchens anywhere, and we also serve Malden and Saugus. Cleaning the trap and flushing with hot water at the end of a cooking session helps in any older kitchen.
