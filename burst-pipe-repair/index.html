When a burst pipe is an emergency

It is an emergency the moment water is flowing and you cannot stop it, or the water is reaching electrical panels, appliances, ceilings or finished floors. A pipe pouring into an unfinished basement corner is still urgent, but a pipe pouring through a living room ceiling is a race. Either way, the first move is the same: shut off the water, then call.

A slow weep from a fitting is a repair for the next available visit. A spray, a gush or a bulging ceiling is a night-and-weekend call.

The first five minutes

  1. 1

    Close the main

    Turn the main shutoff clockwise until it stops. In many homes it sits near the meter in the basement. A quarter-turn ball valve closes with a short swing of the handle.

  2. 2

    Drain the line

    Open a cold tap at the lowest level of the house and another upstairs. That relieves pressure and drains water still sitting in the pipes.

  3. 3

    Cut the power if wet

    If water is near outlets, light fixtures or appliances, shut off the breaker for that area, but only when the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water.

  4. 4

    Photograph and protect

    Take pictures of the damage and the pipe before you move anything. Then lift valuables, rugs and boxes away from the water.

If the water heater is in the affected area, leave it alone until a plumber checks it. A tank that drains while the burner or element stays on can be damaged.

Where pipes tend to fail

Old galvanized steel

Galvanized pipe rusts from the inside out. The wall gets thin, then a section lets go with little warning. If one run has burst, the neighbors are usually close behind, so ask about repiping when you call.

Supply valves and connectors

The stop valve behind a toilet, under a sink or at the water heater can crack, seize or snap when someone turns it. Braided hoses on washers and faucets burst at the crimp. These are the quickest repairs and the most common.

Frozen runs

Water expands as it freezes and splits the pipe, usually in an exterior wall, an unheated basement corner or a crawlspace. The split often does not leak until the ice thaws.

Fittings after other work

A joint disturbed during a water heater swap or a remodel can fail days later. If it happened soon after plumbing work, tell us who did the job and what changed.

Frozen pipes in a New England winter

A frozen pipe is not yet a burst pipe, and the next hour decides which one you get. If a faucet gives only a trickle or nothing in cold weather, the line may be iced over.

If you suspect ice

Leave the faucet open so water can flow as it thaws. Warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer on low, working from the faucet toward the frozen section. Never use a torch or open flame, since it can ignite framing and crack the pipe.

If it has already split

Close the main, drain the system and call. Once the ice melts, the split can release a lot of water, and it may be inside a wall. We cut out the damaged section and replace it with new pipe and fittings.

Prevent a repeat

Insulate pipes along exterior walls and in unheated spaces, seal drafts where lines enter the house, and open cabinet doors on bitter nights. Disconnect hoses and close the interior valve to outdoor spigots before the first hard freeze.

Temporary fix or permanent repair

Temporary stop

  • Clamp or sleeve over a pinhole or split
  • Gets water back on tonight
  • Fine for a day or two in a pinch
  • May fail again under pressure

Permanent repair

  • Damaged section cut out and replaced
  • New fittings sized for the pipe material
  • Pressure tested before the wall is closed
  • Right call when the pipe wall is thin or corroded

We stop the water first, then tell you honestly which one the pipe in front of us needs. If one burst on old steel is a warning that the rest is thin, you will hear that before you approve anything.

If you cannot close your own valve

Some valves are buried, stuck or missing. The shutoff at the street is usually the water department's, so ask the city who handles it at your address. Do not force a valve with tools. Tell us when you call and we will talk through your options.

Water may look rusty or cloudy after the city flushes its mains, which the DPW did citywide from September into late October in 2025. That is a discoloration issue, not a burst. A burst pipe shows up as flow or a wet surface, so check for water where it should not be before blaming the street.

Stay safe around a burst

Never touch a wet outlet or appliance, and keep clear of ceilings that sag or bulge. Standing water over a floor can hide a hot cable. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility before anything else.

Hot water lines can scald. If the burst is on a hot run, stay back until the water heater is off and the line has cooled.
