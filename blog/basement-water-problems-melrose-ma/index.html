Wet basement: when it is plumbing and when it is waterproofing, Melrose, MA

A wet basement is a plumbing problem when the water comes from a pipe, a fixture, a drain, a sump pit or the sewer line. It is a waterproofing or drainage problem when the water comes through the foundation from the ground outside. The quickest test is to check where and when the water appears, and whether it shows up when the weather is dry.

We are a plumbing company, so we fix the first group and tell you honestly when the second group needs a different contractor. This guide helps you sort the cause, shows what each type of leak looks like, and covers the Melrose sewer and water facts worth knowing.

Sort the cause in four checks

  1. 1

    Check the weather

    Water that appears only after heavy rain or snowmelt usually comes from outside. Water that appears on a dry week points to a pipe or fixture.

  2. 2

    Read the water meter

    Turn off every fixture and appliance, then watch the meter dial or low-flow indicator. If it moves, you have a leak somewhere on the property.

  3. 3

    Look at the location

    Wet along a wall base after rain is exterior. Wet near a floor drain, laundry, water heater or stack is more often plumbing.

  4. 4

    Smell and taste the clue

    A sewage smell or gray, dirty water points to a drain backup. Clear water with no smell may be groundwater or a supply leak.

If the meter moves with everything off, treat it as a plumbing leak first. A supply line leak can run for weeks inside a wall or slab before it shows.

A general guide. Some basements have more than one cause at once.

Mixed cases are common. A sump pit that works fine can still overflow if the drain tile is clogged, and a foundation crack can sit next to a pinhole leak in a copper line.

Plumbing causes people miss

Pinhole leaks in copper, a failed washer hose, a weeping water heater fitting and a leaking toilet supply are the quiet ones. They drip for days and often get blamed on rain. Check the walls near the pipes, the ceiling below bathrooms and the base of the water heater.

Sewer and drain causes are louder. A clogged main line pushes water up through the lowest drain, often a basement floor drain or laundry tub. If you see backup after heavy rain, the cause may be the lateral, the public main, or a combination. A camera run through your line can show which part is failing, and a sump pump that works can still be overwhelmed when the ground is saturated.

Melrose note: the sewer program and your basement

Melrose began working on infiltration and inflow in 2016 with a citywide flow metering program using 30 meters for 10 weeks.

Infiltration is groundwater leaking into sewer pipes, and inflow is stormwater entering through connections. The city reports 74 miles of sewer mains, about 57 percent of them and 500 manholes inspected by camera. It also reports over 50,000 linear feet of lining and 19 manholes rehabilitated. More than $9 million has gone into this work, funded through an MWRA grant and loan program.

What it means for you: the public main may be improving, but the city's work is on the public mains, so a problem inside your house or on the pipe serving it still needs its own check. The city's code includes sections titled Separating surface water from wastewater, Infiltration and inflow mitigation fees, and Discharge of clean water to sanitary sewers. Ask the city how these apply to your sump or downspouts before you connect anything to the sewer.

The city also ran citywide water main flushing in 2025, from September 8 into late October. Flushing can cloud the water or knock sediment loose. If you find actual water inside, check your own pipes. If you see actual water inside after flushing, check your own pipes.

Sewage in the basement is a health issue

Backed-up sewage carries bacteria. Keep kids and pets out, wear gloves and boots if you must enter, and wash well afterward. Porous items that touched sewage usually cannot be saved.

If water is rising near the electrical panel or you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility from outside.
