Emergency Hydro Jetting in Melrose, MA

A line that fills up again after every snaking is a line with buildup a cable cannot remove. Our 24/7 line takes calls for kitchen and sewer lines that have backed up again, including at night. Until a plumber arrives, stop using water on that level and keep the floor drain area clear.

Hydro jetting is a drain cleaning method that sends water at high pressure through a nozzle to scour grease, scale and roots off the inside of the pipe. It cleans the pipe wall instead of poking one hole through the clog.

Repeat backups and grease or root buildup

A cable machine opens a path, but it often leaves a film on the pipe wall. That film catches the next round of grease and debris, which is why some lines back up every few months. Jetting washes the film away, so the pipe is close to its full diameter again.

Grease

Kitchen lines cool the fat that goes down the drain, and it hardens into a layer. Hot water alone does not move it far, but a jet nozzle strips it from the wall.

Roots

Fine roots enter through joints and thicken into a mat. A jet with the right nozzle cuts them back. Roots that regrow point to a joint problem, which a camera can confirm.

Scale and sludge

Older lines gather mineral scale and soap sludge that shrink the pipe over years. A line that drains slowly even after clearing is a candidate.

Snaking versus hydro jetting

Cable or snake

  • Opens a hole through the blockage
  • Fast for a first clog in a branch line
  • Leaves grease and scale on the walls
  • Lower cost per visit

Hydro jetting

  • Washes the full pipe wall
  • Better for roots, grease and repeat clogs
  • Needs a sound pipe and a cleanout or access point
  • Higher cost per visit, fewer repeat visits

Most first-time clogs are cleared by drain cleaning with a cable. Jetting is for the line that keeps coming back, the long run to the street or a kitchen line that builds grease.

Where jetting fits in a Melrose house

Kitchen drain line

A kitchen line that backs up every few months usually carries grease. Jetting from a cleanout resets the pipe wall, so the next clog is a long way off.

Main line to the street

A long run of old pipe with roots or scale is the classic job. The jet works the full length instead of stopping at the first soft spot.

Multi-unit and shop lines

Buildings with several kitchens or baths put a lot of fat and paper into one line. These lines often need jetting on a regular schedule.

What a jetting visit looks like

  1. 1

    Look first

    A camera goes in before any pressure. It shows the pipe material, the clog and any cracks.

  2. 2

    Set up at the cleanout

    The plumber connects the jetting hose at a cleanout or through a pulled fixture, with drains blocked to keep water from splashing back.

  3. 3

    Jet toward the clog, then back

    The nozzle works forward, breaking the blockage, then pulls back along the wall to scour it.

  4. 4

    Confirm with a second camera pass

    The result is checked on screen, and anything that jetting cannot fix is shown to you.

When we will not jet

High-pressure water can finish off a pipe that is already failing. A collapsed section, a crushed pipe or a thin-walled old line can break open under jet pressure, so a camera pass comes first.

If the camera shows a break, the plan changes to sewer line repair. Jetting waits until the pipe is sound, and you see the footage that explains why.
